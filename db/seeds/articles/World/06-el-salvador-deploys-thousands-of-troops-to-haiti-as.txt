---
title: "El Salvador Deploys Thousands of Troops to Haiti as President Bukele Launches Aggressive Crackdown on Gangs"
trending: 2
---

El Salvador's President Nayib Bukele has launched an aggressive military intervention in Haiti, deploying more than 2,000 troops to combat powerful gangs that have devastated the Caribbean nation.

The operation intensified following an October 18 ambush that left four Salvadoran aviation technicians missing while serving with an international security mission.

Bukele, widely known for his controversial crackdown on gangs in El Salvador, has vowed to confront Haiti's armed organizations.

In a recent joint operation, Haitian police and Salvadoran soldiers destroyed a property linked to Wilson Joseph, leader of the notorious 400 Mawozo gang. However, the gang leader escaped capture.

Haitian Prime Minister Alix Didier Fils-Aimé has welcomed international security assistance, emphasizing that foreign troops must operate under Haitian authority.

Despite growing optimism among some Haitians, the intervention has raised serious concerns about national sovereignty, civilian safety, and potential human rights violations.

Critics have also questioned the legal framework governing Salvadoran forces operating outside the United Nations-backed security mission.

As Haiti continues battling widespread violence, political instability, and a humanitarian crisis, one question remains: Can Bukele's hardline approach restore stability to Haiti, or will it escalate an already devastating conflict?
